# Password and MFA policy

**Company:** Lab (a fictional company used for a lab)
**Owner:** IT lead · **Review:** once a year

## Why we have this
Most break-ins start with a stolen password. This policy makes that much harder, and it supports the Cyber Essentials user access control requirements.

## Who it covers
Everyone with a company Microsoft 365 account: staff, contractors and temps.

## Passwords
- Make it at least 12 characters. Three random words strung together works well and is easy to remember.
- Use a different password for every account. Never reuse your work password anywhere else.
- Keep passwords in the approved password manager or your browser's built-in one, not on sticky notes or in a spreadsheet.
- You don't need to change your password on a schedule. Change it straight away if you think someone else knows it.
- Never share your password with anyone, IT included. IT will never ask for it.

## Multi-factor authentication (MFA)
- MFA is on for every Microsoft 365 and cloud account. No exceptions for staff.
- Use the Microsoft Authenticator app. Text message codes are a backup only.
- If an MFA prompt appears and you didn't just try to sign in, say no and tell IT straight away. It usually means someone has your password.

## Admin accounts
- Admin rights go only to the people who need them, and only the rights they need. For example, the person who manages users gets User Administrator, not Global Administrator.
- Admins sign in with MFA every time. Admin accounts are not used for everyday email or browsing.
- We keep one emergency ("break-glass") account for the day normal admin sign-in fails. It has a long, unique password kept offline in a safe place, plus a passkey or hardware security key. It is the only account excluded from the MFA policies, it is never used day to day, and any sign-in with it is checked.

## When someone leaves
Follow the [leavers checklist](leavers-checklist.md) on their last working day.

## Reporting
Lost phone? Strange prompt? Think you clicked something you shouldn't have? Tell the IT lead straight away. You won't be blamed for reporting it. Reporting late is the real problem.
