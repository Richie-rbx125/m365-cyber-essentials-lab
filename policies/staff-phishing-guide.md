# Spot it, stop it, report it: phishing in 60 seconds

Phishing is a fake message that tries to get you to click, pay or hand over a password. It can come by email, text, phone or Teams. Anyone can be fooled, so this is about catching it quickly.

## Five warning signs
Don't rely on typos or odd grammar. AI-written scams can look perfect, so check what is being asked, not how well it's written.

1. **Pressure.** "Pay today or your account closes", "urgent", "final warning".
2. **Something you weren't expecting.** Invoices, delivery notes, shared files or "voicemail" attachments.
3. **The sender doesn't add up.** The name looks right but the address doesn't. Hover over it to check.
4. **A request to change bank details**, for a supplier or for your own pay. Always confirm these by phone.
5. **An MFA prompt you didn't ask for.** Someone may already have your password.

## What to do
- Don't click, reply or open attachments.
- If it says it's from someone you know, check with them using a phone number you already have, not one from the message.
- Report it to the IT lead, or use the Report button in Outlook.
- Clicked something by mistake? Tell IT right away. A fast report matters much more than blame.

## Remember
IT will never ask for your password or your MFA code.
